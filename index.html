About me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at Villanova University, advised by Prof. Qianhong Wu, and a Visiting Scholar at Thomas Jefferson University. My research brings together biomechanics, fluid mechanics, and experimental and computational methods to understand how medical devices interact with soft biological materials.

My current work focuses on deep brain stimulation (DBS): how probe insertion deforms tissue, how insertion channels evolve, and why electrodes deviate from their planned trajectories. I combine force measurements and imaging with analytical and numerical models, and collaborate on studies connecting patient-specific brain mechanics with clinical electrode deviation. Beyond DBS, I study flow and deformation in compliant tubes and contribute to research on nanoparticle transport and deposition. Across these projects, I use experiments and models together to understand the mechanics behind the observations.
