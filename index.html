About me

I am a Ph.D. candidate in Mechanical Engineering at Villanova University, advised by Prof. Qianhong Wu. My research centers on biomechanics and biomedical engineering: understanding how medical devices interact with soft biological materials, fluids, and particles. I combine experimental mechanics, computational modeling, medical imaging, and data-driven analysis to study these interactions under controlled conditions.

A central focus of my work is deep brain stimulation (DBS), including probe insertion forces, channel deformation, and electrode deviation in brain-mimicking gels. My research connects force measurements and high-speed imaging with analytical and finite element models of probe–tissue mechanics. I also study fluid–structure interaction in compliant PDMS tubes and particle transport and assembly on polymer substrates, with broader interests in soft-material mechanics and biomedical devices. I hold an M.Sc. in Mechanical Engineering from The Hong Kong Polytechnic University and a B.Sc. in Theoretical and Applied Mechanics from Southern University of Science and Technology.
